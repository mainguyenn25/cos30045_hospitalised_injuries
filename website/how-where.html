How and where

How people were travelling, what they collided with, and whether the crash happened in a city or out in the regions.

Pedestrians are rarely hospitalised, but most likely to die

Car drivers are the biggest group by far. Pedestrians make up 7% of cases yet a quarter of in-hospital deaths, and stay in hospital nearly twice as long as the average patient.

Hit by a car, cyclists are far more likely to die

Most cyclists and motorcyclists are hurt in crashes that involve no one else. But when a cyclist is hit by a car or van, the stakes change: those crashes are 21% of cyclist injuries and half of cyclist deaths. For drivers, hitting a fixed object such as a tree or pole is the deadliest crash type.

Crashes outside the cities are more severe

Two in three cases come from major cities, but a regional crash puts the patient in hospital for longer and is more likely to end in death.
